Scope: Nigeria only in this pass. Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, Egypt and Côte d'Ivoire regulation: Not researched.

## CBN FX Manual, Fourth Edition (2026)
- Issued May 2026, "effective 1 June 2026", replacing the 2018 Manual. Source: https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-foreign-exchange-framework-reformed-key-insights-on-the-cbn-foreign-exchange-manual-2026
- "Foreign Currency" now "expressly includes digital notes or coins". Authorised dealer status extends beyond banks to any CBN-licensed financial institution authorised to deal in FX, which "may include fintechs". Same source.
- Reported changes: advance import payments from 15% to 30% of FOB value; tuition remittances up to USD 25,000 per semester; "tougher compliance rules". Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/financial-services/1806166/key-regulatory-update-in-nigeria-the-cbn-foreign-exchange-manual-2026 , https://brandspurng.com/?p=193114 (search summaries, not opened).
- Not found: import documentation rules, export proceeds handling, penalties and reporting detail (the page I opened covered only definitions).

## CBN circular on IMTO settlement accounts
- Dated 24 March 2026, effective 1 May 2026. IMTOs must open naira settlement accounts at authorised dealer banks, route all transactions through them, and keep them credited only with remittance flows and FX conversion proceeds. Pricing must follow "real-time market prices from the Bloomberg BMATCH". Source: https://nairametrics.com/2026/03/25/cbn-orders-imtos-to-open-naira-settlement-accounts-with-nigerian-banks/
- Penalties: Not found.

## IMTO licensing regime (2024 guidelines)
- Foreign applicants need minimum share capital of US$1 million; local IMTOs the naira equivalent; application fee raised from NGN 500,000 to NGN 10,000,000; two stages (Approval-in-Principle, Final Approval); daily, weekly and monthly returns to the CBN. Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/fin-tech/1422812/a-new-regime-for-international-money-transfer-operators-license-in-nigeria , https://techpoint.africa/news/cbn-reviews-imto-licence-application-fee/
- IMTOs limited to inbound transfers; banks and fintechs barred from holding an IMTO licence (reported Feb 2024). Source: https://nairametrics.com/2024/02/02/cbn-bans-banks-fintechs-from-international-money-transfer-services-hikes-application-fee-by-1900/
- Relevance to Tabs, INFERRED: Tabs describes outbound payouts and licensed partners; the inbound-only IMTO limit and partner-licence model are worth a compliance read. No source links the two.

## CBN payments framework circular (15 June 2026)
- Covers banks, microfinance banks, PSPs, mobile money operators, switching companies. Requires disclosure of ultimate beneficial owners of significant shareholders; a 25% consumer-issuing / 15% merchant-acquiring market share cap; monthly market share returns with "full compliance expected by 31 December 2026"; Nigerian payment data stored locally, "Compliance is required by 1 January 2027". Source: https://resource.scp-law.com/2026/07/22/beyond-compliance-what-the-cbns-new-payments-framework-means-for-governance-competition-and-transactions/
- Capital requirements: Not found in that summary.
