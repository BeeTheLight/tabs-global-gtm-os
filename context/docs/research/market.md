## Nigerian tax changes (2026)

- The Nigeria Tax Act (NTA) and Nigeria Tax Administration Act (NTAA) took effect 1 January 2026. Source: https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0447-nigeria-tax-registration-for-nonresident-companies-undergoing-subtle-but-significant-shift-in-nigerias-international-tax-landscape (EY, 2026-02-17; read in full)
- Non-resident companies without a permanent establishment or significant economic presence may now be expected to register for tax. NTAA section 4 "requires every taxable person to register with the relevant tax authority"; section 147 defines taxable person without reference to physical presence. EY calls the reading an interpretation. Same source.
- Penalty: NTAA section 100(2) imposes "statutory penalty of 5,000,000 Nigerian naira (NGN5m) for awarding contracts to unregistered persons"; EY says it "may apply per contract and potentially per unregistered vendor". "Unregistered person" is not separately defined. Same source.
- Withholding tax on offshore services paid to a non-resident is final tax absent a PE or SEP (NTA sections 17(3)(b) and 17(4)). EY states no rates. Same source.
- Search summary (not read, Guardian returned HTTP 403): VAT on foreign suppliers' taxable supplies; the tax authority can appoint collection agents including digital platforms and payment processors; PE royalty deductions restricted; foreign-incorporated entities managed from Nigeria treated as Nigerian. Sources: https://guardian.ng/?p=2779091 ("2026 tax reset: What cross-border payment companies must know"), https://guardian.ng/business-services/nigeria-introduces-stricter-vat-rules-for-digital-services-foreign-suppliers/ , https://news.bloombergtax.com/tax-insights-and-commentary/nigeria-reforms-tax-laws-for-2026-rewrites-rules-for-mnes
- KPMG flagged "errors and gaps" in the new acts (2026-01-06). Source: https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/2026/01/Inherent%20Errors,%20Gaps%20and%20Ommissions%20in%20New%20Tax%20Acts%20-%206%20January.pdf
- INFERRED: Nigerian businesses paying foreign vendors now have a reason to check vendor registration and keep payment records; whether this creates demand for a payments provider is not stated by any source.

## CBN Foreign Exchange Manual, 4th edition (2026)

- Issued May 2026, effective 1 June 2026; 21 memoranda; replaces the 2018 version. Source: https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/nigeria-cbn-releases-forex-manual-4th-edition-pegs-pilgrimage-pta-at-4-000-per-quarter-b6348fo9 (search summary); manual PDF: https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/The%20new%20Forex%20Manual%20-%204th%20Edition.PDF (index and cover only were returned, Memorandum 9 not read)
- Reported changes: advance import payments from 15% to 30%; import shortfall and excess tolerance of ±10% of CFR value on Form M; authorised dealers fined N100m plus N10m per transaction for inadequate documentation; daily returns by 10 a.m.; third violation brings a 90-day market suspension; "Authorised Dealer" widened to any CBN-licensed financial institution dealing in FX. Sources: https://nairametrics.com/2026/06/06/cbn-to-fine-banks-n100-million-for-inadequate-forex-documents/ , https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/financial-services/1815108/the-cbns-2026-foreign-exchange-manual-key-reforms-and-practical-implications-for-businesses (search summaries)

## CBN directive to IMTOs (naira settlement)

- Circular dated 2026-03-24, signed by Musa Nakorji, Director, Trade and Exchange Department. IMTOs must open naira settlement accounts with authorised dealer banks and route disbursements exclusively through them; recipients paid in naira; effective 1 May 2026; sanctions for foreign-currency payouts or bypassing the accounts. Sources: https://nairametrics.com/2026/03/25/cbn-orders-imtos-to-open-naira-settlement-accounts-with-nigerian-banks/ and https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/03/26/cbn-directs-imtos-to-open-naira-settlement-accounts-to-deepen-diaspora-inflows/ (search summaries)
- No reporting found of an extension.

## CBN payments framework circular (15 June 2026)

- Reference PSS/DIR/PUB/CIR/001/004. Full compliance by 31 December 2026 on market structure: an institution above 25% share in consumer issuing within a rolling 12 months cannot exceed 15% in merchant acquiring; monthly market-share returns. Data localisation: payment transaction data generated in Nigeria must be stored and managed in Nigeria by 1 January 2027. Beneficial ownership disclosure also required. Scope covers banks, microfinance banks, mobile money operators, PSPs and PSSPs. Sources: https://regalert.today/document/ffc123b8-cb7d-4767-8857-b79bebce2a39/text , https://businessday.ng/technology/article/cbn-ends-offshore-payment-processing-as-banks-fintechs-face-2027-data-localisation-deadline/ , https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/cbn-data-localisation-and-market-structure-rules.pdf (search summaries; circular text itself not read)

## Digital assets and stablecoins (Nigeria)

- The SEC has moved toward a formal VASP and stablecoin regime in 2026: January 2026 circular 26-1 on minimum capital with a 30 June 2027 compliance deadline; foreign stablecoin issuers need a local representative and Nigeria-specific reserve rules; a VASP track for stablecoins, payments and settlement was reported open 12 to 31 August 2026. Reported capital figures conflict (N200m, N300m and N2bn appear in different sources). Sources: https://aluko-oyebode.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Nigeria-Digital-Asset-Regulatory-Reset.pdf , https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/regulation/nigeria-erects-strict-stablecoin-wall-demanding-11m-local-subsidiaries-for-us-dollar-tokens/ (search summaries)
